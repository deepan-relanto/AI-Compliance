The one-sentence version

Before pasting anything into an AI tool, ask: is this tool approved for this type of data, and am I sharing only what the task truly needs?

Ease of use is exactly what creates the risk — a careless paste can move confidential data outside protection in seconds, with no alarm and no undo.

Workplace stakes

Incidents are real. Regulators are watching. You are the control.

Incidents are real

Major companies restricted chatbots after employees pasted confidential source code and meeting notes into public tools. Productivity was real — so was the leak.

Regulators are watching

GDPR, PDPA, CCPA and similar laws apply fully to data handled through AI. “It was an AI tool” is not a defense.

Trust is the product

Customers and partners share data on the promise it will be protected. One careless prompt can break a promise that took years to build.

You are the control

Firewalls cannot read your intentions. The decision about what goes into a prompt is made by you — every time.

3.1 Data classification

Know what you are holding — then match the tool

ClassWhat it coversAI rule of thumb
PublicWebsite content, press releases, public reportsGenerally safe with approved tools
InternalOrg charts, memos, process docs not for outsidersApproved tools only, per policy
ConfidentialStrategy, financials, contracts, customer lists, source codeOnly tools cleared for confidential data; share the minimum

When unsure, treat it as the higher class. Misjudging upward costs minutes; downward can cost a client or a finding.

Personal data & contracts

Identity and contracts change the rules

Personal data

Any information about an identifiable person — names, IDs, photos, performance notes, customer records, or combinations that identify someone indirectly.

Sensitive personal data

Health, financial details, government IDs, biometrics, children’s data — the strictest protection under most privacy laws.

Two AI rules

Minimize: if the task does not need identity, remove it. Check the basis: using data in an unapproved tool or for a new purpose can breach privacy law even if nothing “leaks.”

Contracts & NDAs

Client material in an unapproved tool can breach confidentiality clauses directly — even if no harm materializes. Convenience does not amend a contract.

3.6 Shadow AI

Unapproved tools put data outside company protection

What it is

Personal chatbot accounts, free “summarize this page” extensions, online converters, transcription sites — tools the organization has not approved.

Why it hurts

Data is governed by an unknown provider’s terms, outside company protection, and invisible to security teams.

The honest path: if approved tools cannot do what you need, request through IT. Days of delay beat months of recovery.

3.7 Safe prompting

Get the value while sharing the least sensitive version

1. Minimum

Paste the paragraph, not the whole contract; the error, not the codebase.

2. Anonymize

Replace names with Client A, Product X, Region 1. Keep your key offline.

3. Generalize

Ask in abstract form — no client details required.

4. Synthetic

Use fictional examples to design templates; apply to real data inside approved systems.

5. Check files

Uploads and screenshots carry hidden tabs, metadata, and stray names.

3.8–3.9 Everyday controls

Protect accounts, integrations, and know which rules apply

Security habits

  • Work account on approved tools only — never personal accounts for work content
  • Treat AI logins like email: unique password + MFA
  • Only IT-cleared extensions; grant least privilege on integrations
  • Label and share AI outputs like any other document
  • Verify payment/credential requests out-of-band — polish proves nothing

Compliance families

  • Privacy laws — purpose, minimal use, approved processing
  • Sector rules — finance, health, public sector constraints
  • IP — protect company IP on the way in; check output rights
  • Contracts / NDAs — AI tools count as processing
  • Internal AI policy — which tools, which data, what review duties

3.10 Patterns to learn from

Well-meaning shortcuts caused the damage

Source-code paste

Proprietary code and notes into a public chatbot. Lesson: use approved channels.

Exposed chat histories

Bugs and breaches exposed conversation data. Lesson: whatever you paste lives in someone else’s system.

Oversharing assistant

Connected tools surfaced files people should never see. Lesson: AI amplifies existing permissions.

AI-polished phish

Fluent, personalized fraud and voice clones. Lesson: language quality no longer proves authenticity.

3.11 Incident response

Speed and honesty matter most

Report immediately

Manager and security/privacy contact. Minutes matter for containment and legal notification clocks.

Do not delete and hide

Concealment turns an honest mistake into misconduct and blocks containment.

Preserve details

What was shared, which tool, when, under which account — that drives the response.

Culture point: organizations that punish honest, fast reporting train people to hide incidents. Quick reporting is the right thing.

Quick reference

Do this — not that

Do

  • Use only approved AI tools with your work account
  • Classify before you paste; match tool to class
  • Share the minimum — excerpts, placeholders, synthetic examples
  • Upload purpose-built clean copies only
  • MFA + unique passwords on AI accounts
  • Verify payment/credential requests out-of-band
  • Report mistakes immediately
  • Request needed tools through IT

Don’t

  • Personal accounts, free sites, or unvetted extensions for work
  • Judge sensitivity by length alone
  • Paste full case files, client lists, or codebases when a fraction serves
  • Upload working files with hidden tabs or metadata
  • Treat AI logins as low-value
  • Trust fluent language as proof of authenticity
  • Delete, conceal, or wait and hope
  • Route around controls with shadow AI

Habits that stick

Avoid the usual traps — build the three-second habit

Common mistakes

  • Convenience paste — whole document because selecting takes ten seconds
  • Wrong-window slip — personal and work tools side by side
  • Attachment iceberg — hidden tabs, comments, metadata
  • Approved-tool blank cheque — approval always has scope
  • Quiet exception — “my case is special” under deadline pressure

Recommended behavior

  • Before every paste: name the data class and confirm the tool is approved for it
  • Keep a placeholder convention (Client A, [NAME], Region 1)
  • Know your incident channel before you need it
  • When teammates take risky shortcuts, say something constructively
  • Separate browser profiles for personal vs work AI
